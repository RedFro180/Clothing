# Decoration supplies, prices and how-to (30/70 hemp/cotton fleece)

Sized for a first batch of ~25–50 hoodies. Prices with a link come from retailer pages (Nov 2026); **(est.)** = estimate. Check live prices before ordering. Cotton and hemp are both plant (cellulose) fibers, so a 30/70 blend is treated like cotton.

## 1. Natural dye (dyeing and printing)

| Item | Use | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Soda ash (1–5 lb) | Scouring (removes oils and sizing) | $12 / 1 lb, ~$24 / 5 lb ([Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/ip/36484015)) |
| pH-neutral detergent | Scouring | ~$10 (est.) |
| Tannin extract | First mordant for plant fibers | $23–98 ([Botanical Colors](https://botanicalcolors.com/product-category/natural-dyes/natural-dye-extracts/)) |
| Aluminum acetate | Second mordant for plant fibers | $18 / 4 oz, $32 / 8 oz, $55 / 16 oz ([Botanical Colors](https://botanicalcolors.com/shop/mordants/aluminum-acetate/)) |
| Madder extract | Reds/pinks; 50 g dyes ~750 g fiber dark red (≈ one heavy hoodie) | $18–140 ([Botanical Colors](https://botanicalcolors.com/shop/natural-dyes/natural-dye-extracts/madder-extract/)) |
| Logwood extract | Purples/greys; black with iron | $21–125 ([Botanical Colors](https://botanicalcolors.com/shop/natural-dyes/natural-dye-extracts/rich-logwood-purple/)) |
| Iron (ferrous sulfate) | Darkens logwood to black | ~$10–20 (est.) |
| Guar gum or gum tragacanth | Thickener for print paste | ~$10–25 (est.) |
| Stainless steel pots, 20–40 qt (×2) | Dye baths | ~$60–150 each (est.) |
| Gram scale, thermometer, measuring cups | Measuring | ~$40 (est.) |
| Burner or hot plate | Heat, outdoors or garage | ~$50–150 (est.) |
| Steamer pot with rack | Fixing prints | ~$50–100 (est.) |
| Gloves, respirator, goggles | Safety | ~$40–60 (est.) |

**Start-up ≈ $450–900. Dye and mordant ≈ $3–8 per hoodie.**

**How to (30/70):**
1. **Scour:** simmer 1–2 hrs in water + soda ash + detergent; rinse.
2. **Mordant:** tannin (~8% of dry garment weight), then aluminum acetate (5–8% of dry weight).
3. **Dye:** heat slowly, hold ~1 hr. Keep madder under ~80 °C or reds turn brown. For black: logwood, then a short iron bath.
4. **Print:** mix extract or mordant with thickener into a paste; screen-print; steam 30–60 min or air-cure ~2 weeks; wash out the thickener ([Dharma/Botanical Colors method](https://dharmatrading.com/html/eng/24959688-AA.shtml)).
5. **Test:** wash test pieces 10+ times before selling.

## 2. Discharge printing (lightening a design out of a dark hoodie)
Works only on hoodies dyed with reactive (Procion-type) dyes, not natural dyes.

| Item | Use | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Procion MX Jet Black (if dyeing yourself) | Black base dye | ~$11–12 per 2/3 oz jar ([Staples](https://www.staples.com/Jacquard-Procion-MX-Fiber-Reactive-Dye-aquamarine-069-2-3-oz-Pack-of-3/product_1717794)); several jars for 25 hoodies (est.) |
| Soda ash | Dye fixer | $12 / 1 lb |
| Jacquard deColourant (formerly "Discharge Paste") | Removes color where printed | ~$7–9 per 8 oz ([Dharma](https://www.dharmatrading.com/products/ddp.html)) |
| Steam iron or heat press | Activates the paste | iron ~$40; heat press ~$200–400 (est.) |
| Respirator with vapor cartridges, gloves | Fumes when heated | ~$40–60 (est.) |

**Get the deColourant safety data sheet before buying in bulk** and confirm it contains no plastic binder. I couldn't confirm its full ingredients. A Jacquard data sheet I found lists a "sulfinic acid derivative" ([SDS](https://dharmatrading.com/images/public/pdf/msds/DDP_2015_SDS.pdf)), but I couldn't confirm which product it's for.

**Start-up ≈ $100–500 + shared screen gear. ≈ $1–3 per hoodie.**

**How to (30/70):** screen-print the paste on the dark hoodie → dry → steam-iron or heat-press until the color lifts (ventilate) → wash. The result is lightened fabric, usually cream or rust rather than pure white. Hemp's slubs soften edges slightly.

**Start-up ≈ $1,300–1,700 with a PE900. ≈ $1–3 per hoodie.**

**How to (30/70):** digitize (use lower stitch density on fleece) → hoop with a cotton backing → stitch → trim backing and threads. Most cutaway backings recommended for knits are polyester; use cotton muslin instead. Cotton thread breaks more often than polyester, so run the machine slower.
