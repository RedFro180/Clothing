# Other products: tees, sweatpants, underwear, socks, hats, heavy jackets

Same rules as the hoodies: natural fibers, **no synthetic polymers** (no polyester, nylon, spandex/elastane, acrylic, plastic trims or plastic-based inks), home decoration, self-shipping, custom fit, and a target gross margin over 50%. Prices are from web research (Nov 2026). **(est.)** = my estimate with no quote behind it. The numbers are in the **Other products** sheet of `margin-model.xlsx` (blue cells are editable). Gross margin includes your time at $20/hr and excludes marketing, as on the hoodie sheets.

**Key facts from the numbers**
- **The 50% target is met with a paid US maker only for socks and caps** (margins on both are small in dollars), and roughly for sweatpants at $150. Tees, underwear and jackets miss it unless made overseas or priced well above market.
- **Overseas full package meets 50% for every product** at the high price, but those per-unit numbers are estimates: I found real quotes only for tees (Pakistan, $8–14 FOB at 50 pcs). Every factory will default to polyester thread, labels and elastic unless your spec sheet forbids them.
- **Sweatpants are the best fit.** They use the hoodie fabric (one fabric order, one supplier), sell as a set, and carry the highest dollars per unit after the jacket.
- **Tees have the worst margin.** Your time (30 min = $10) and small-batch sewing are a large share of a $45–60 price.
- **The jacket needs ~$10,600 in cash for a 50-piece first run** (US maker) and carries the most fit and quality risk. It's a poor second product.
- **Gym buyers:** cotton holds sweat and gets heavy when wet. Most gym apparel is synthetic because it dries faster. Sell plastic-free as the reason to buy, not as better gym performance.

## 1. Short-sleeve tees

**Fabric:** hemp/cotton jersey, 6–7 oz/sq yd (200–240 GSM) for a heavyweight feel. 55/45 hemp/cotton is the blend most stocked in jersey: Dharma $8.82–10.79/yd at 61" wide ([Dharma](https://www.dharmatrading.com/fabric/55-Hemp-45-Cotton-Jersey-61.html)); Bulk Hemp Warehouse 6.5 oz washed $9.56–12.74/yd ([BHW](https://bulkhempwarehouse.com/bulk-hemp-fabrics/hemp-knit-fabrics/jersey-knits)). 70% cotton / 30% hemp jersey is also sold ([Organic Cotton Plus](https://organiccottonplus.com/products/jersey-59-natural-70-oc-30-hemp)), but it's organic, so pricier. Hemp jersey feels drier and stiffer at first and softens with washing; for first-wear softness pick 30/70.
- **Collar:** 100% cotton 1x1 rib. With no spandex the neckline stretches out over time. Use a heavier rib and a cotton twill neck tape.
- **Thread:** cotton serger thread works, but breaks more often than polyester. Stretch-test seams.
- **Making it:** easiest garment to sew yourself (~1 hr with the hoodie's serger + coverstitch). US small-batch sewing $4–8 basic, roughly double for small runs ([MakersRow](https://makersrow.com/blog/cost-of-manufacturing-clothes-price-quote/)); I used $10. Pakistan heavyweight tees run $8–14 FOB at 50 pcs ([Tradewheel listing](https://www.tradewheel.com/mens-t-shirts/)) and $10 at 100 pcs ([Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/garments/pakistan-t-shirt-suppliers-p2127-c126)). Those are listings, not quotes.
- **Decoration:** the best canvas for natural-dye screen printing (flat, light, absorbent). Print cut panels before sewing. Discharge needs reactive-dyed tees. Embroidery puckers on light jersey; keep it small.
- **Price check:** premium heavyweight 100% cotton tees sell around $50 (e.g. [Abercrombie](https://www.abercrombie.com/shop/am/p/long-sleeve-premium-heavyweight-20-tee-63136024), imported); US-made brands include [American Giant](https://www.american-giant.com/products/mens-heavyweight-t-silent-storm) and [Los Angeles Apparel](https://losangelesapparel.net/collections/women/products/1203gd-unisex-short-sleeve-binding-garment-dye-t-shirt).

## 2. Sweatpants

**Fabric:** the hoodie fleece (30/70 hemp/cotton, 330–400 GSM), ~1.6 yd per pair (est.). Cuffs: heavy 100% cotton rib.
- **Waistband (the hard part):** almost all waistband elastic contains spandex or polyester. Plastic-free options:
  1. **Cotton-covered natural rubber elastic.** Sold mainly by EU suppliers at 100 m minimums ([supplier list](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1OUFOAgZNfheOBiuGfhEo7RJIUZD3VmLmzFTKP7E2eWo/edit?gid=0)); retail cotton/rubber elastic exists (Dritz, [Joann](https://www.joann.com/cotton-swimwear-elastic-natural/11384435.html)). I assumed ~$2/yd (est.).
  2. **No elastic:** a wide cotton rib waistband with a cotton drawcord. Holds less well on heavy fleece.
- **Natural rubber trade-offs:** it contains latex. Latex allergy affects ~1–6% of the US population (estimates vary; [review](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/joh/58/2/58_15-0275-RA/_html)), so state it on the product page and label. It also loses stretch faster than spandex with dryer heat, so tell buyers to tumble low or line dry. I found no US rule requiring a latex warning on apparel, but disclose it anyway. Rubber is an FTC generic fiber name, so check 16 CFR 303 for how to list the elastic on the fiber label.
- **Making it:** similar effort to the hoodie (~1.5 hr self-sewn, est.). US small-batch sewing $22 (est.; guides say $22–38 for small runs). Pakistan: no sweatpant quote found; tracksuits are listed at $10 FOB at 100 pcs ([Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/garments/pakistan-t-shirts-suppliers-p2127-c126)), so I used $17 landed (est.).
- **Decoration:** a down-the-leg or hip print (print the panel before sewing), or hip embroidery.
- **Price check:** LA-made heavyweight sweats like Les Tien list at about $175–225 ([SSENSE](https://www.ssense.com/en-ca/men/sale/designers/les-tien)).

## 3. Underwear

**The core problem:** spandex is what makes briefs and boxer briefs hold their shape. Without it, knit underwear bags out during the day.
- **Most practical plastic-free build: woven 100% cotton boxers** (loose fit, no recovery needed) with a natural-rubber waist or a cotton drawcord. **Knit boxer briefs** work with natural-rubber elastic at the waist and legs, but they'll be less snug than spandex blends.
- **Brands doing this:** Rawganique boxers $30–42, with an option of 50% organic cotton / 50% natural rubber elastic ([Rawganique](https://rawganique.com/collections/mens-organic-underwear/products/organic-cotton-twill-boxers-whitby)); Q for Quinn sells a cotton + natural-rubber elastic version and warns it's unsuitable for latex allergies ([Q for Quinn](https://www.qforquinn.com/products/everyday-knit-boxers-100-organic-cotton-bio)). Several "plastic-free" brands still sell elastane versions, so check fiber lists before you use them as fit references.
- **Fabric:** lightweight cotton or hemp/cotton jersey (~160–200 GSM, est.) for knits; cotton poplin or twill for woven boxers. ~0.4 yd per pair (est.).
- **Making it:** fast to sew (~30 min with a serger, est.). Grading and fit matter more than on any other item. Plan 2–3 sample rounds.
- **Decoration:** a small natural-dye print on the seat or leg. Avoid embroidery (scratchy). Branded waistbands are normally jacquard-knit synthetic, so print a cotton waistband instead.
- **Returns:** I found no US law requiring you to accept returns of worn or opened underwear; final sale for hygiene is common ([FindLaw](https://www.findlaw.com/consumer/consumer-transactions/customer-returns-and-refund-laws-by-state.html)). You must post the policy clearly (Florida §501.142 if you're in Florida), and defective items still get refunded.
- **Packaging:** sealed paper or glassine bag, not poly.

## 4. Socks

**You can't sew socks.** They're knit as a tube on sock machines, and the design must be knitted in. Home printing on socks normally uses sublimation (polyester only). Natural-dye prints distort when stretched and wear off at the heel.
- **Plastic-free trade-offs:** without nylon, heels and toes wear through faster. Without spandex, socks slide down. Wool holds shape better than cotton. Rawganique sells elastic-free 100% merino and 100% organic cotton socks and says plainly they're less snug ([Rawganique](https://rawganique.com/collections/elastic-free-organic-cotton-linen-hemp-merino-socks)). Some of its socks still contain nylon or "1% biodegradable elastane", so check each one.
- **Factories:** standard mills stock nylon/spandex yarn, so a fully natural spec may raise price and minimums. Known US minimums:
  - Fort Payne, AL mills: ~144–288 pairs per style, ~$1.80–2.20 for a standard crew sock ([Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/alabama-socks-wholesale), one reseller blog)
  - DeFeet: 60 pairs ([DeFeet](https://defeet.com/pages/defeet-custom2))
  - DeadSoxy knit-in: 100 pairs, $5.27–7.47 ([DeadSoxy](https://deadsoxy.com/blogs/custom-socks/custom-sock-moq-explained))

  I assumed $7/pair for plastic-free custom (est.).
- **Home sock machine:** computerized machines from China run ~$2,800–4,000 ex-factory ([Made-in-China](https://m.made-in-china.com/hot-china-products/Computerized_Sock_Machine_Price.html)), plus shipping, setup and a steep learning curve. Not recommended in year one.
- **Role:** a cheap add-on that raises order value. Margin % is high, but it's only ~$7 per pair after fees and shipping.

## 5. Hats

**Beanie (best hat for this brand)**
- 100% cotton or 100% merino knit. Ribbed knit holds without elastic. Factory-made with low minimums: Aung Crown from ~25 pcs, JoinTop from 100 ([JoinTop](https://jointopcap.com/?p=8754)); Loopper organic cotton ~€4.25 at 250 pcs ([Loopper](https://www.loopper.com/personalized-winter-hats/personalized-knitted-winter-hats/knitted-hat-merino-wool-6467654)). Most cheap beanies are acrylic; specify 100% natural yarn and cotton labels.
- **Decoration:** embroider the cuff on your PE900 (hoop with cotton backing; the knit stretches, so test), or sew on an embroidered cotton patch. A knitted-in design needs the factory.

**Dad cap (unstructured cotton twill)**
- 100% cotton unstructured blanks cost ~$3–4 ([Buck Wholesale](https://www.buckwholesale.com/blank-dad-hats-unisex-cotton-polo-unstructured-low-profile-baseball-caps-with-buckle-back-closure-15-colors-5223)). But the "100% cotton" usually means the shell only. Sweatband, thread, labels, brim board and closure are often polyester or plastic. Brim inserts are normally plastic or cardboard, so ask for cardboard. A fully natural cap means a custom factory spec: cotton sweatband, cotton thread, cardboard brim, metal buckle. I assumed $8 (est.).
- **Decoration:** a PE900 can't embroider an assembled cap; cap frames come with multi-needle machines like the $12,000+ PR680W. Use a sewn-on embroidered patch, or have the factory embroider the front panel.

Pattern, grading and samples ~$2,000 (est.).
- **Decoration:** duck is the best surface for a large natural-dye back print (flat, woven, absorbent). Print the back panel before sewing. Chest embroidery also works.

## Rules that apply to all six
- **FTC labels:** fiber content, origin, your name or RN, and a care label. Rubber elastic and hosiery have specific rules in 16 CFR 303; check them.
- **Factory spec sheet:** list every component and the required material: thread, labels, elastic, interlining, brim board, zipper tape, sweatband, packaging. "100% cotton" on a quote usually means the main fabric only.
- **Tariffs** for Pakistan and Portugal are uncertain; check hts.usitc.gov per product code before ordering.
- **Wash-test** every product 10+ times, especially natural-rubber elastic and cotton-thread seams.

## Order to launch (by margin, cash and risk)
1. **Sweatpants:** same fabric as the hoodie, set sales, ~47% US / ~74% overseas at $140.
2. **Beanie:** low minimum, cheap, uses your embroidery machine, high margin.
3. **Tee:** cheapest entry price for buyers, but low margin unless made overseas or priced $55–60+.
4. **Socks and caps:** add-ons; socks need ~$1,300 in cash per design at a 144-pair minimum.
5. **Underwear:** only after you've tested natural-rubber elastic and fit; final-sale policy.
6. **Jacket:** last; ~$10,600 in cash for 50 US-made pieces, an industrial machine if self-sewn, and the hardest plastic-free trims.
